# The Generation Procedure

The repeatable steps the crew runs to produce narrative content, tied to the agents in [`../gdd/11-ai-agents-and-pipeline.md`](../gdd/11-ai-agents-and-pipeline.md). Procedural technique comes first; cohesion and story serve it as guardrails (`guardrails.md`). The pipeline runs in four phases and never mixes them: arc and beat map (steps 1–5), then scene graph (steps 6–7), then scaffold (built by the resolver — `build-loop.md`), then lines (step 8); steps 9–13 (cross-life state, verify, purge, QA, gate) follow (efficiently-branching-narrative.md). The Narrative Architect (Agent 1) owns the first two phases; the Content Agent (Agent 2) owns only the last.

> **Two numbering systems, and confusing them ships the wrong work.** This file numbers **13 pipeline steps**. [`../plans/2026-08-03-storyline-authoring-process.md`](../plans/2026-08-03-storyline-authoring-process.md) numbers a **7-step authoring process** for running one soul, one thread at a time. They do not line up: *authoring step 5* (author each conversation) is *pipeline step 6*, and *authoring step 6* (lines) is *pipeline step 8*. Always say which — "authoring step 6 / pipeline step 8", never a bare "step 6".
>
> **The Architect split (Roc, 2026-08-04) is not yet reflected below.** Step 6 says the Architect authors the branch intent. That is now the **Choice designer**'s ([`../agents/choice-designer.md`](../agents/choice-designer.md)): the Architect owns the card, arc, threads, thread shape, dependency order and how many conversations a thread gets; the Choice designer owns conversations, gates, options, outcomes and fallbacks. Read every "the Architect authors the node / the branch intent" below as the Choice designer.

**1. Steering.** Write or refresh the arc doc (`steering-layer.md`). Nothing generates without it.

**2. Intake.** Four inputs: the arc doc; Roc's roster of NPC seeds (the Architect cannot invent a soul); a backstory guideline per soul (D&D arc doc: NPC postures); and the player-chosen background. The background seeds the player's own backstory and a proposed player arc, the way a D&D player hands the DM a backstory (D&D arc doc: player spines). The pipeline steers that arc with the softest mediation only: surface a clue, shift a neighbor, offer a lead. It never fails a player action to herd them back onto the proposed arc, and it never buries the arc so it cannot surface; both stronger mediations are push and are barred by name (procedural-narrative-generation.md). Intake resolves the current state into a bag of plain facts and structural scores: confirmed essence descriptors, knowledge held, world state, festival year, the reshuffle role map, the hidden bond score. Nothing felt ever enters the bag (Fallen London / StoryNexus). Every scene request leaves Intake tagged with the one World Truth it serves, the Arc Question, any stale thread it should move, and the soul's arc-spine note if hand-seeding. A request with no steering tag bounces back to Intake; no scene generates without reference to the arc layer. When the roster is set, Intake emits the NPC codex: the canonical list of which souls exist and their locked facts, built once and read by the Verifier every batch so no worker can invent or contradict a soul (neverendingquest-ai-dm-architecture.md, the NPC codex). The codex indexes each soul's locked facts to the places and threads they touch, the coherence data `guardrails.md` check 4 reads against. **The codex is universal, not per-arc (ruled 2026-08-09 — Roc), and it is now a real file: [`npc-codex.md`](npc-codex.md).** One codex serves every arc; each entry records the arc it originated in, so the town's lore accumulates in one place instead of fragmenting per run. Until 2026-08-09 the codex was specified in ten documents and existed in none — check 4 was reading against nothing, which is why invented offstage people passed silently.

**Walk-ons are a codex class, not souls.** A scene sometimes needs a functionary to carry business — a runner delivering a message, a stallholder quoting a price. **Prefer an existing soul first:** the Giver's third-party notice line went to Juno rather than an anonymous villager, which cost no new character and put the observation on someone it suited. When a walk-on is genuinely needed, the codex records it as `walk_on` — no essence, no card, no arc, no bond — existing only to carry the business of one scene. Without the class they accrete: a named functionary reused across scenes quietly becomes a soul nobody decided to author, and the roster grows past what §6.3 and the budget allow.

**A walk-on has no card, so nothing set its voice — and that was a real defect until 2026-08-06.** Length and warmth vary per soul *by the card*, so an uncarded character fell through to the world dialect, which is the tightest register in the game, and villagers came out sounding like the guarded protagonist. **Walk-ons now take the walk-on band** (`register.md`): longer, warmer, unguarded, and explicitly **exempt from deflect-not-name** — they may say plainly that they were pleased or worried, because they have no unsaid thing for the player to work out. A `walk_on` codex entry may narrow the band for one functionary; absent an entry, the band is the default.

**Reuse-before-invent is general, not a walk-on rule (ruled 2026-08-09 — Roc).** The Juno precedent — put the third-party notice on an existing soul rather than mint an anonymous villager — holds for every class of invention: offstage people a line mentions, world facts, geography a route runs past. Before any seat invents, it checks whether an existing codex entry can carry the reference; an entry that fits costs nothing and thickens someone already authored. The codex is what makes that check possible — reuse-before-invent was unenforceable while there was nothing to read — and inventions that survive the check enter through the declaration loop: the Content seat declares them typed (`agents/content-dialogue.md`), the Verifier routes them to the gate as PROPOSE (`agents/consistency-verifier.md`; `guardrails.md` check 12), Roc ratifies, the codex records. Invented texture is wanted; the loop exists so it accumulates on purpose instead of leaking in.

**3. Cards.** The Architect fills each seed and its backstory guideline into a persona_card (schema in `templates/persona-card-schema.md`). The essence_descriptor is the soul in one paragraph — one want, one behavior cluster, stated as want and repeated action, never as a job (creating-strong-characters.md; modular-characters-system-driven.md). **It is the compressed card summary step 8 re-pins into every call**, so its overlap with the other essence fields is intentional; a contrast to the player's arc is optional and valid only when the player's mere presence creates the collision, never when it takes an act (ruled 2026-08-03 — Roc; `templates/persona-card-schema.md`). Trait axes are orthogonal by rule: deflection target, precision profile, and warmth channel are independent, and a value on one never predicts a value on another, because correlated traits are what make a generated cast read as one archetype in different hats (voice-style-guide.md; modular-characters-system-driven.md). The axis set is kept deliberately wide and each axis set independently, so variety comes from combination rather than from more archetypes (modular-characters-system-driven.md); these fixed trait values double as coefficients that weight bond accrual (step 9), never as accruing state of their own. Each card also carries a voice_register, stable across reshuffles; one conviction no bond state can buy out (npcs-with-agency-80-days.md); and a note on what this soul notices and wants, written before any line exists (romantic-narrative-design-kindness-coins.md). The soul's arc spine is not a card field; it lives in the arc doc as a human note that guides which facets to surface early versus late (`steering-layer.md`). Five to ten percent of the roster is authored_exceptions: handwritten souls that break these rules, marked on the schema so the Verifier reads them as sanctioned (modular-characters-system-driven.md). Scope stays honest: a few deep souls carry full state, the rest run lighter (narrative-lego-ken-levine.md).

**4. Echoes.** The Architect writes echo_templates (schema in `templates/echo-template-schema.md`): seed_scene, seed_event under 25 words, payoff_scene, payoff_condition. The seed sits beside plot-inert material, a chore, a price, a pest, so it cannot be told from noise when planted (when-great-foreshadowing-goes-unnoticed.md; e02.md). It names a thing the player can picture, a worn path, a drafty seat, because a vague seed cannot be recognized later (environment-as-visual-storytelling.md). The payoff_condition is a named deduction the player must already hold: a recognition condition, never a promise about feeling. Turn shapes come from the Frieren templates: the cut-off with a deferred gap, the logistics-first reveal, the motif that recurs near-verbatim in a heavier register (frieren-dialogue-jp.md). Echoes tier by inference load, surface, mid, hidden, with placement carrying the tier and no difficulty label (curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds.md). Each template is conditioned on history, so the same template pays off differently depending on the soul's card, the accrued bond, and the role they held last life (Wildermyth). Two bounds hold: the seed-to-payoff ratio is tracked at arc level, because seeds that never land train the player to stop looking (when-great-foreshadowing-goes-unnoticed.md), and every seed scene must work on its own for a player who never returns (when-a-scene-is-more-tragic-than-you-realized.md). A payoff that reaches into a soul's past must recontextualize something present, or it is a dump and fails its worth check (the-problem-with-backstory.md).

**5. Delta and canon.** The Architect sets the delta_rule. A scene carries two unlike things, and **only one of them is rationed (ruled 2026-08-03 — Roc; supersedes the old WORLD/PERSONAL count)**. `delta_situation` is what the scene is dealing with — the dough went flat, coal is up a coin. It is public, true whoever walked in, and it is what pushes the story forward; **it is not counted and not capped.** `delta_cast` is the rationed thing: a soul fact getting more specific, meaning a possession, a habit, or a line they will not cross (D&D arc doc: NARRATIVE_NOTES.md). "The player feels closer" is not a cast fact and never fills a slot. `delta_relational` is the third type: true only because two souls are in the room, bound to the pair rather than to either soul, and it does **not** travel across lives because the reshuffle re-deals the pairing. One per scene. **Floor: one new thing per delta scene — cast fact, situation, or relational. Ceiling: two cast facts in a solo scene, or one per cast member present plus one relational in a shared scene, hard — and the Architect may not raise it to fit its own content; a request to exceed it surfaces to the human gate as a decision** (`guardrails.md` check 3). A scene that cannot fit its beat inside two cast facts is a scene-spec problem, not a ceiling problem. The cast facts arrive under cover, and the situation is that cover: a scene's surface job is a favor, a joke, a small conflict, and a scene whose only job is exposition is the failure mode (in-praise-of-layered-exposition.md). **Reference is free**, and this is a slot rule rather than a text rule: any established fact or situation may be spoken about, acted on, or built on in any later scene, as often as the story needs, and none of that is a delta. Threads stay connected exactly that way. What is flagged is a **delta slot holding something already delivered and unchanged** — the slot declares what the scene adds, so old news in it means the scene adds nothing. That is a **structural** flag, routed up to the Architect to be re-specced, never down to Content to be re-worded. Situation is stateful: dough flat → starter begged off a neighbour → still twelve short is three situations, not one restated, and situation is uncapped so a thread can advance as often as the story wants. Whether a change is real or merely cosmetic is a human-gate call, not a Verifier check (ruled 2026-08-03). When a past choice resurfaces after the year skip, its consequence shows as a callback line and a changed piece of world, never a signposted right answer (quest-design-lessons-witcher-cyberpunk.md). A quiet beat carries no slots at all and is a valid, scheduled beat type, not a gap to fill; it is exempt from the floor, never from the ceiling (in-pursuit-of-a-perfect-montage.md). canon_flags lock what must not drift, including world facts bound to a soul's ID, so an established fact travels with the soul into their next role and never passes to whoever now holds the old seat (side-characters-to-worldbuild.md).

**6. Graph before prose.** The scene graph goes up greybox first: one named node per scene beat, placeholder lines carrying full branch logic, no finished prose (narrative-games-yarn-spinner.md). The authored-vs-generated line is drawn explicitly: the Architect authors the scene spec and the branch intent; code builds the node IDs, the gating conditions, the zero-information fallback, and the backups, so the LLM never hand-assembles structure (neverendingquest-ai-dm-architecture.md, its 8-step module builder is the running template). Content the player has seen gates later content automatically, so every prior beat is a future condition without a hand-kept flag table (narrative-games-yarn-spinner.md). Scenes assemble from small preconditioned pieces rather than fixed trees, each piece gated on world state and knowledge state, each safe to enter in any order with a zero-information fallback, so significance survives being found first, last, or alone (Fallen London / StoryNexus; heavens-vault-detective-story.md). Encounters, not quests: errands generate from world state by triangulating three inputs, what fits this place, what the arc doc needs surfaced next, and what the player has not been offered lately, and the output is always two or three live leads, never one marker (heavens-vault-detective-story.md). This is the road refused on purpose: a scripted quest tree with next-point pointers is the concrete alternative (neverendingquest-ai-dm-architecture.md, its plot_schema) that atomic-encounter assembly replaces. A pacing layer above the pool biases which pieces surface, and only biases; it never forces a beat the player did not walk toward (RimWorld). The errand vocabulary stays small and hand-curated, or triangulation produces junk (heavens-vault-detective-story.md).

**7. Recognition gates.** Every essence-side gate, the deduction the player can win, is specced in five fields: Problem, Circumstance, Clues, Solution, and The Idea, one plain sentence of intent that survives the trip from Architect to Content Agent to Verifier to Roc (myst-techniques.md). The proof is diegetic: an action only someone who understood would choose, never a button that says "I figured it out" (narrative-deduction-mechanics.md). The gate is built as an argument assembled piece by piece, so wrong guesses walk back and teach, and "inconclusive, come back when you know more" is a legitimate outcome, not a failure (narrative-deduction-mechanics.md). Gate and verbs are co-designed: one of the four verb families, Collect, Make, Use, Converse (Show and Ask are tag-layer names under Use and Converse), must be able to carry the proof, or the gate collapses into a menu. The families are the game's own verb grammar, defined in [`../gdd/03-core-loop.md`](../gdd/03-core-loop.md) and [`../gdd/04-magic-system.md`](../gdd/04-magic-system.md); the co-design rule comes from storytelling-with-verbs.md. The verb set locks after the first festival year; stakes escalate across the three years, the verbs do not, and the run's retrospective resolves through the deduction the player already practiced (mechanics-elevate-narrative-oxenfree.md).

**8. Lines, one slot per call.** The Content Agent receives one prepared slot: persona_card, echo_template, scene_context, an assigned tone from the fixed enum (quiet, wistful, matter_of_fact, warm, distant), voice_register, `slot_type` (`dialogue` | `action` | `object` | `player_line`, which sets the word ceiling and decides what the slot may legally contain — see `guardrails.md` check 8), and max_words.

> **Two named patterns this step runs on (2026-08-08).** **Plan-validate-execute:** the Content Agent writes its `speaker_intent` *before* the line and then writes the line to satisfy it, so the intent is an input a line can be built toward rather than a caption describing whatever came out. **Validator loop:** `node tools/card-lint.mjs` runs before dispatch, fails on a defect, gets fixed, runs again. Both are general shapes, not one-offs — reach for them when adding a seat or a check rather than inventing a new mechanism.
>
> **What may be pinned (added 2026-08-08).** Two card fields ride into every call: `essence_descriptor` and `voice_register`. Both are exemplar-grade prose and nothing else qualifies. **`voice_enforcement` is never pinned** — it holds the length bands, warmth temperature and failure modes the Verifier tests against, and a generator handed a checker's vocabulary writes to avoid flags instead of writing to sound like a person (`templates/persona-card-schema.md`, the split and its hygiene rules). The orchestrator pins the in-voice half; step 12 reads the enforcement half. Run `node tools/card-lint.mjs` before any generation pass: it fails on verdict vocabulary, word budget, or em-dash ceiling in a pinned field.

> **Who assigns the tone — settled 2026-08-06, because nobody did.** The Content Agent "assigns no tone of its own", and neither the Architect brief nor the Choice designer's output carries one, so the field had no author. **The orchestrator assigns it when preparing the slot**, reading it off the Choice designer's content block for that node — the block already says what is open, what is closed and what the soul reveals, which is what a tone is chosen from. It is a preparation decision, not a creative one, and a slot arriving at Content without a tone is a preparation defect, not something Content may fill in.
>
> **Deeds are executable.** An option is spoken (`player_line`) or a deed (`surface_action`) — `ink.ts:386` throws if it is neither. `slot_type: action` and `object` both appear in live data. The claim that "no `action` slots exist" (Toby's card; `../plans/2026-08-03-storyline-authoring-process.md`, What this changes in v01) was true on 2026-08-03 and is **stale** as of 2026-08-06.
>
> **One slot per call prevents drift and causes convergence. Nothing here reconciles a batch.** (Found across three prose passes, 2026-08-06 — see `GP-119`.) The re-pinning and the threshold-and-compress above exist to stop a soul drifting *within* a batch, and they work. The same isolation means each writer solves the soul's signature move **once**, with no view of what the others wrote — so four writers each invented one supply sentence for Toby and used it about ten times. Measured: pass one read as four different writers; fixing that with a shared voice reference produced six sentence constructions shared across four files; banning those six by name produced fourteen, the top one firing nine times. **Distinct staging vocabularies are not sufficient** — two of four held.

> **Local-model call shape and model pick (added 2026-08-25, from local-model testing).** When step 8 runs against a local model instead of the production model: always call the API directly with a system+user split — a chat-UI's own template wraps the prompt and produces flat, generic output that never reproduces card-specific voice. Output shape is the validated hybrid: one short third-person narrator beat, then the spoken line in quotes, kept as two separable pieces so a downstream step (the ledger update, ink packaging) can isolate the line alone. The beat's words are additive to `max_words`, not counted against it. **Model pick, current as of 2026-08-25:** `gemma4-26b-fiction-bf16` (MoE, Q4_K_M, `--moecpu 999` expert offload) is the clean-sweep result — no copying, no format breaks, no invented canon, no truth-guard-adjacent leak, across both rounds of testing. `Gemma-The-Writer-Mighty-Sword-9B` Q6_K is the reliable, faster second choice, also clean on every axis. Any other local model needs **Q5_K_M minimum** for a 12B-class model before its output should be trusted — every model tested was materially worse at Q4. Full battery: `pipeline-runs/2026-08-17-register-loosening/2026-08-24-local-model-findings.md`, `assignments/assignment-8-icm/_kobold-tests/round2-findings.md`.
>
> **Resolved 2026-08-10: the gap is a seat.** It recurred on the second soul exactly as predicted — a nine-thread batch put roughly half of Ilsa's warmth beats in Toby's channel, ran all six of Mara's warm beats off one template, and used Toby's licensed shortfall-then-arithmetic in Mara's thread on the same number. Neither adjacent step could have caught it: **step 8 can fix convergence and cannot see it**, working one slot at a time; **step 11 can see it and is forbidden to fix it**, flagging only. Roc minted the [**batch reconciler**](../agents/batch-reconciler.md) on that second instance — it reads a finished batch, holds every slot's job fixed, and varies only what repeats. **It rewrites**, which is a bounded exception to the flag-only discipline and the reason it needed Roc's word rather than a seat's. It runs after Lines completes a batch and before Roc's gate, never mid-batch and never on one file.
>
> **A repeated construction is a batch defect and no single-slot call can see it.** Step 11 can see it and is forbidden to fix it; this step can fix it and cannot see it. The 2026-08-06 remedy was a **batch-scoped revision under step 13** — one Content instance takes the finished batch with the construction counts, holds every slot's job fixed, and varies only what repeats. Whether that becomes its own seat is open (`GP-119`); do not mint one on a single instance.
>
> **The receiving pair — a two-slot floor, added 2026-08-06.** "1–3 response slots" reads as a range and the first shelf batch took the floor: seven of thirteen options in one conversation got a single response. That is what produced prose Roc called cold and choppy, and the cause is mechanical rather than stylistic. **A soul's flat line and its warm line are a pair.** Toby's receiving lines are one to five words by design; the warmth arrives in the *second* slot, where he anticipates something and supplies it. Cut the second slot and all that survives is the clipped half, which reads brusque — the warmth-invariant defect, arrived at by omission rather than by wording.
>
> So: **any option where the soul is receiving — being thanked, noticed, given to, or having attention turn back on them — takes at least two response slots.** One flat, one carrying the anticipation. Aim for about 2.5 responses per option across a conversation; one-slot options are for beats where the soul is *giving*, and they are the exception. A conversation averaging under 2 is a preparation defect, not a style preference.

**Player choice is expressible via the choice_node unit** (`templates/choice-node-schema.md`, specced 2026-07-29; closes spec change 7 from the 2026-07-25 run). Branch structure lives in the scene graph, never in `content_lines` — the array stays flat, and items gain optional `choice_id`/`option_id` back-references. The Architect authors the node in step 6 (options, player_verb, state_actions, the two guard notes); this step fills the node's line slots one per call: each `player_line` (the words the player says, ≤12 words, player entry in `register.md`) and each response slot (the soul answers the player's specific words, 1–3 lines). The two constraints from the gate ruling are enforced by `guardrails.md`: neither branch may be the correct answer (check 10), and repeated helping must not accumulate into an unlock, because being claimed is not a reward for niceness (check 2). It fills a chosen turn scaffold rather than free-writing from a blank page (frieren-dialogue-jp.md), substituting card values into the scaffold's named slots so the suit and essence signature run through every line the soul speaks (procedural-history-caves-of-qud.md). Each output line carries a speaker_intent note: what the line means from the speaker's side, care routed through maintenance, grief surfacing as a price list (dialogue-of-hades.md). The agent makes no structural decision and assigns no tone of its own. Invention runs on the declared loop (ruled 2026-08-09 — Roc; `agents/content-dialogue.md`): check the codex first, invent where nothing fits, declare what was invented by type; the Verifier routes legal new inventions to the gate as PROPOSE and only ratified ones enter `npc-codex.md`. A card's own traits and locked facts stay untouchable, and no line may contradict a codex entry. Its context is rich and its output is compressed: the card carries far more than any line states, and the line spends the fewest exact words (how-to-write-absurdly-well-tchaikovsky.md). Against drift over a long batch, the orchestrator re-pins the compressed card summary — which **is** the `essence_descriptor` field, authored once rather than recomputed per call (`templates/persona-card-schema.md`, ruled 2026-08-03) — and the voice_register block into every call, and runs a named threshold-and-compress on scene state: at every ten accumulated slots the oldest are folded into one running summary that is then re-pinned, a bounded-context rule rather than an open-ended history (neverendingquest-ai-dm-architecture.md, its chronicle compression). The register these lines obey is in `register.md`.

**9. Cross-life state.** Each soul has a canon store that persists across lives and reshuffles: essence_descriptor, signature, locked facts. The dead sister stays dead in every life. **The unit is a *life*, never a "run"** — the game's time model has no runs (see [`../gdd/06-world-and-progression.md`](../gdd/06-world-and-progression.md)), and run-language in a spec the crew reads is how it reaches shipped content. A soul is never regenerated fresh per life; a reshuffle deals a new role and a subset of the soul's authored facets from its pool, essence fixed (narrative-lego-ken-levine.md). Confirmed essence descriptors live where they belong, in the player's head and a diegetic almanac the player fills, re-asserted against the same soul across lives as the player's own act (visual-novel-design-virtues-last-reward.md). The per-soul bond score accretes silently, host-side, from time spent, help given, festival choices. Each contributing action produces one delta to that single count, weighted by two inputs: the action-category it falls under, from a small hand-set (Trust, a promise or secret kept; Intimacy, shared time, disclosure, presence; Recognition, the player notices and remembers a soul across a reshuffle). **Respect was retired 2026-08-10 (Roc) and folded into Trust and Recognition** — craft shown is a promise kept in another form, and a soul's values honoured is that soul noticed and remembered. The evidence for the fold: nine threads were designed end to end across three souls by writers working independently, and **not one node recorded Respect** — every bond delta landed on the other three. Respect is earned by *doing something well* or acting on a stated value, and a point-and-click hub where the player chooses how to be near someone rarely offers that shape of option. Do not re-add it without content that needs it, and the soul's fixed card-trait coefficients, so a guarded soul's bond moves slower (modular-characters-system-driven.md). Each category is reachable only through a verb family, because the count records what the player did, not what the soul feels. The count is never split into per-category stored sub-scores; that would rebuild the quantified-emotion model this pipeline refuses (neverendingquest-ai-dm-architecture.md, its Companion Memory Core, is the concrete contrast; the guard is `guardrails.md` check 2). It has no UI. No hearts, no meter, no "bond deepened" event, no quest-log line naming a want. It surfaces only as behavior: a changed line, a door left open, a soul that could not hold back. The threshold beat is the soul's own act, never a payout for niceness (romantic-narrative-design-kindness-coins.md). Wants read as behavior and dialogue; the motive underneath stays open, a surface the player projects onto, while the feedback to any specific player action stays concrete (emergent-storytelling-the-sims.md). At the retrospective the accreted score selects among the several endings; it is never printed, and it never fires one mandated scene (narrative-games-yarn-spinner.md). Where this state lives (host code, not ink) is in `build-loop.md`.

**10. Verify.** The Consistency Verifier (Agent 3) reads each batch against the invariant set in `guardrails.md` and against every soul's canon store. This includes the coherence gate the replacement grammar needs: a grammar-filled line tends to explain itself after the fact, so session self-consistency is checked, not assumed (procedural-history-caves-of-qud.md). The Verifier flags only. It never rewrites (dev-crew-architecture.md).

**11. Purge the tells.** Before the human gate, a mechanical editing pass runs on every batch: em-dashes out, banned words scanned, summary openers cut, passive voice flagged, and a specificity check, every clause names a thing the player can picture or the clause goes (environment-as-visual-storytelling.md). It flags like the Verifier; it does not rewrite.

**12. QA.** QA (Agent 5) runs reachability, soft-lock, and dead-end checks over the scene graph, plus spot checks that a wrong action teaches (non-linear-narratives-horizon.md). Flags only.

**13. Escalate, then gate.** A flag on prose returns to the Content Agent for revision inside the same canon, a capped retry with model fallback allowed on a repeated failure (neverendingquest-ai-dm-architecture.md, its capped retry-with-fallback loop). A flag naming a structural cause, a bad echo_template, an essence contradiction, routes through the orchestrator up to the Architect as a new prepared input; workers never call each other. Roc approves every line at the output (`review.md`). The gate does not move mid-chain.

**The revision cap — two per item, for every worker, and it does not reset at the gate.** The cap counts Architect structural round-trips as well as Content returns, and it applies to any flag type, not only prose. Anything still flagged after the second revision rides to the gate with its flag attached; a third attempt is a decision Roc makes, not one the orchestrator takes. **This is a budget control as much as a process one:** the 2026-07-25 run spent 79% of its tokens on revisions — 3.8× the generation they corrected — because the old cap bound only prose flags to Content and reset once the batch passed a gate.

**Every revision brief restates the full constraint set, never only the defect.** Given a single-axis instruction, the Content Agent reliably fixes that axis and silently spends another constraint: told to fix an invented fact it lost the essence signature; told to fix flow it lost the echo's canon; told to fix warmth it lost the refusal; told to fix self-narration it lost the offer's target. Four for four in one run. The only pass that traded nothing away restated the whole constraint set alongside the defect and asked for a self-check against it before returning. **A brief that names only the problem is an instruction to trade something else for it** — so briefing quality, not the cap, is what actually keeps revisions rare.
