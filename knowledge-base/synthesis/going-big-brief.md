# Going-Big Brief — Three Experiments, and Where Each Applies

> **Note (post-restructure, 2026-07-26).** "Build GDD section N" citations below use the pre-v5 three-doc structure-model numbering (game-30/32 era) and do not match the current structure. See [`../../gdd/CONTEXT.md`](../../gdd/CONTEXT.md) for where this content lives now.

**How to use this.** This is the going-big trigger model behind [`../../gdd/09-art-direction.md`](../../gdd/09-art-direction.md) and [`../../gdd/03-core-loop.md`](../../gdd/03-core-loop.md) (the festival-outcome spectrum's rare top tier). It does **not** name a winning pole. Roc's D1b left the
"big" register **open to experiment** [`GATE-2-review.md` D1b; `RESYNTHESIS-PLAN.md` §2],
so this brief presents all three poles — **Outer Wilds REVELATION**, **Ghibli AWE**,
**Frieren RESTRAINT** — as live experiments, states the one rule that lets a swell coexist
with the voice guide's "amplification destroys it," and gives Phase 3 a routing table for
which experiment leads at which beat. Read §1 for the three experiments, §2 for the
reconciliation (the load-bearing call), §3 for the trigger taxonomy, §4 for the
where-each-applies routing, §5 for how it renders in a cozy static-scene P&C, §6 for the
Phase-3 dials that stay open, and §7 for the honest gaps. The companion `going-big-brief.COMPARE.md`
holds the side-by-side reasoning; this is the brief the writer builds from. **Nothing here
is locked** — the poles stay experimental and the routing is a recommendation.

## 1. The three experiments — what "big" can mean here

Roc's D1 read: the tournament/combat episodes were never about violence; they gave the
show **contrast** (quiet default → rare spectacle) and an **outlet to go big**. Keep the
function, drop the violence — the tool of going big becomes understanding and awe, not
power [`GATE-2-review.md` D1; D1c kills combat mining]. Three experiments answer *what*
that outlet is. They are not rivals; each owns a different **layer** of the same moment,
and any single going-big beat can run one, two, or all three at once.

### 1.1 Experiment A — Outer Wilds REVELATION (the *mechanism* layer)

"Big" is the cognitive detonation when a season of small, ordinary observations snaps into
one vast understanding [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`]. Outer Wilds' spine —
"knowledge is how you progress" — is already installed in this game: the Teledahn pattern
("one elegant observation gates the whole downstream chain") and the Laki travelling cipher
("knowledge learned anywhere is usable everywhere") are the same engine
[`pnc-grammar` §2.2, §2.5]. The bigness is what the player *carries into* the moment, not
what the game adds at it. Retrospective significance is the same mechanism expressed as
prose register: "an ordinary detail becomes meaningful in hindsight … a quiet surface is
what lets a small thing detonate later" [`voice-style-guide` §4].

**Signature discipline — the preload list.** A revelation moment has three parts, all
authored in advance, none happening *at* the payoff: the **preload** (small, seeming-noise
observations distributed across scenes and years), the **convergence** (the player acquires
one more piece and the prior observations snap into alignment — this happens inside the
player, "pull, never push"), and the **acknowledgment** (what the game does at convergence —
minimal). Experiment A's authorization gate: *before any going-big marker is placed, name
the preload.* Which observations, across which years and scenes, has the player been
accumulating? No preload list, no revelation — only performance
[`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`; `voice-style-guide` §4].

### 1.2 Experiment B — Ghibli AWE (the *visual amplitude* layer)

"Big" is emotional amplitude in an ordinary frame — warmth, scale, a tableau that earns the
word *sublime* [`ghibli-animation-secrets`]. The Ghibli discipline: "treat the mundane with
the same care as the spectacular — the equality of attention is what makes the fantasy
believable" [`ghibli-animation-secrets`]. A bowl of soup rendered with full attention can
carry more than a spectacle sequence, because the attention itself declares worth. This is
the warm register Roc's D1 explicitly wants — "warmth rather than OW's cosmic dread"
[`GATE-2-review.md` D1] — and it fits the cozy pillar.

**Signature discipline — four visual levers + the keystone image.** In a static P&C scene
the levers are: **saturation as scene-register** (high = safety/wonder, low = threat/loss,
baked into layout not a runtime filter) [`secret-colors-of-studio-ghibli`]; **color as
relationship** (warm dominant for protagonist/allies, shared palette elements signalling
silent kinship across scenes) [`secret-colors-of-studio-ghibli`; `frieren-color-design-eyes`];
**ambient life** (dust in light, cloth movement, a door left ajar — the details that keep a
background from reading as flat illustration) [`ghibli-animation-secrets`]; and ***ma***,
the designed pause — "the space between actions, the moments of stillness that allow tension
to build and emotions to settle" — at least one held, non-interactive, composed-anyway area
per scene [`ghibli-animation-secrets`; `frieren-backgrounds-analysis`]. The scheduled swell
gets a **keystone image** — one gesture sketch of what arriving here should *feel* like,
built before any other asset in the scene; "if it can be fully described in a sentence, it
hasn't found its essence yet" [`essence-of-art-direction-mtg-ghibli`].

### 1.3 Experiment C — Frieren RESTRAINT (the *register* layer)

"Big" is a vast scale made legible through stillness — the swell earned by everything that
did not swell before it. Three mechanisms make it work: **stillness as accumulation**
("long sequences of still or slow background … letting the world breathe" — the banked quiet
that a reveal detonates against) [`frieren-true-beauty-retrospective`]; **compositional
scale without narrative announcement** (lingering wide shots that establish the world
continues beyond the frame — "far more wide shots that linger for just a moment") not to
dramatize but to imply depth [`frieren-animation-breakdown`]; and **restraint as the
condition of impact** ("the quieter the default register, the more those motion beats land")
[`frieren-hopeful-masterpiece`]. This is the native-fit experiment: it is the same spine the
voice guide already runs, at the visual level.

**Signature discipline — quiet default + the specificity check.** The whole game's baseline
is ambient, observational, visually restrained. A swell is a *composition shift, not a
score*: wider frame, a distinct landmark now accessible, ambient sound held. But restraint is
not a licence to under-design — the honest risk is that "still very good but competent
detailed fantasy" produces a held moment that is pleasant, not revelatory
[`frieren-backgrounds-analysis` Watch-out]. So Experiment C's gate: *before drawing any
scene's backgrounds, answer 3–5 "what does X actually look like here" questions*
[`frieren-animation-breakdown`]. A background that passes the specificity check can afford to
be quiet; one that doesn't must not hide behind restraint.

## 2. The reconciliation — a swell that does NOT destroy the payoff

This is the sharp tension Roc named at GATE 2, and the load-bearing call of the whole brief.
The voice guide says: *"weight is preloaded, not performed — amplification at the payoff
destroys it"* [`voice-style-guide` §4]. D1a permits a swell at narrative/reward payoffs
[`GATE-2-review.md` D1a]. How do they coexist?

**The rule this brief adopts (the layer-split):**

> **A going-big swell is permitted when three conditions hold at once:**
> **(1) a named preload earned it** (Experiment A's gate) —
> **(2) the amplification lands in the visual / spatial layer** (a composition that opens, a
> palette that lifts, a space the player stands in) —
> **(3) the text / voice layer stays silent or flat at that beat** (no announcement, no
> recap, no "this changes everything," no synchronized swelling line).
> **Visual swell + understated-or-silent text = the permitted mode. A narration line, a
> "this matters" beat, or musical amplification stacked on an already-amplified visual = the
> failure mode.**

Why this holds, drawn from all three experiments:

- **The rule targets *announced* importance, not *acknowledged* weight** (Experiment A). The
  §4 prohibition fires on the game *deciding* a beat should feel big — swelling the music,
  lingering the camera, having a character say it changed everything. A sanctioned swell is
  the opposite: it is an *acknowledgment that the preload is complete*. The event is the
  player's own felt convergence; the swell only marks it. Timing and authorship are the
  difference [`voice-style-guide` §4].

- **The channels are different** (Experiment B). "Amplification through the voice layer
  destroys it; amplification through the visual layer is the game." The voice rule governs
  dialogue, narration, text. The visual layer "carries what words are poor at — the vibe, the
  world feeling, emotional texture" [`essence-of-art-direction-mtg-ghibli`]; the player's eye
  receives it without the mediation of language, which is why it can swell where the voice
  cannot. In the Frieren adaptation the two layers deliberately *diverge* — the camera lingers
  on backgrounds "enough time for us to get an idea of what life might be like" while
  characters say almost nothing [`frieren-animation-breakdown`]. The layers alternate, each
  working in the silence the other leaves.

- **"Swell" means compositional opening, not volume** (Experiment C). The permitted swell is
  a held background composition slightly wider and more open than anything the scene showed
  before — the reward-space becoming visible *is* the swell [`myst-techniques` 3.10;
  `pnc-grammar` §2.2]. The final tableau states the theme as a physical arrangement, "the
  emotional beat is spatial, not spoken" [`myst-techniques` 2.9]. *Ma* — a held still with
  ambient sound and no narration — is a larger gesture than a score hit
  [`frieren-backgrounds-analysis`]. This is also why the P&C medium is a *fit*, not a
  limitation: the player controls pacing and self-creates the *ma* beat by pausing — provided
  the background rewards the pause.

**The fallback, named honestly.** The layer-split is an *interpretation* of §4 as a *text*
rule, not a quote from it. If Roc reads §4 as a **total-register prohibition** (no
amplification in any channel), the "permitted swell" in D1a reduces to Experiment C's
understated-but-vast reading: the swell is *only* a compositional opening — a wider frame, a
newly visible landmark, a held *ma* — with **no** saturation lift and **no** visual
amplitude beyond "more open than before." That fallback is fully buildable and costs nothing
to adopt; it simply removes the Experiment-B saturation-lift lever from the payoff beat. The
brief specifies both readings so GATE 2 can pick one. **Everything else in this brief is
invariant across the two readings** — only the visual-amplitude lever at the payoff changes.

## 3. The trigger taxonomy — two tiers all three experiments share

All three experiments converge on the same two-tier split, which is the structural output
for §7. The tiers differ by *frequency, cost, and whether they pay off at the moment of
encounter* [`GATE-2-review.md` D1a].

**Tier 1 — Payoff swell.** Rare. Tied to a narrative or reward gate. Authorized by a named
preload (A), delivered as a composed visual/spatial opening (B) held in the quiet register
(C), with the text layer silent or flat. Allowed to hold the scene momentarily, then resumes
at the default altitude. Composition is per-scene: **LARGE-SCALE** (wide view, changed
landscape, spatial scope) or **INTIMATE-ZOOM** (close object, a specific particular thing) —
a scene-composition instruction to art direction, **not a hierarchy of weight**
[`GATE-2-review.md` D1a].

**Tier 2 — Wonder seed.** Frequent. Near-zero cost. **No payoff at the moment of
encounter** — its job is to produce a *live question* in the player's mental ledger (A: the
preload engine in action), or to prove the world is real by rendering the mundane with full
care (B: ambient awe), or to hold a background worth sitting in (C: object specificity +
off-frame implication). It should not feel like a reward; it should feel like a world bigger
than the current frame. The Outer Wilds ship-log discipline applies: each seed gives the
player exactly the *question*, not the answer — "distilled to only what the designer is 100%
certain the player knows from that one piece of text"
[`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`].

**The tier discipline (all three experiments agree):**
- Every Tier-1 trigger carries a **preload list** field — named prior observations (which
  scenes, which years) that earned this detonation. Empty field = not yet authorized (A).
- Every Tier-1 trigger carries a **composition flag** — `LARGE-SCALE` or `INTIMATE-ZOOM` (D1a).
- Every Tier-1 payoff-swell location carries a **keystone image** built before the scene's
  other assets (B), and must **pass the specificity check** (C).
- The **seed-to-detonation ratio** is an explicit dial, not a maximum. Too many unresolved
  seeds reads as noise and trains the player to stop looking; Phase 3 tracks which seeds
  correspond to which eventual payoff [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds` Watch-outs;
  `pnc-grammar` §3 diegetic-noise ratio].

## 4. Where each experiment applies (the routing table)

This is the §7 trigger model's routing table. For each D1a context it names the **lead**
experiment (the layer doing most of the work at that beat) and the **support**. This is a
*recommended distribution*, **not a pole lock** — D1b keeps the register open, so Phase 3 can
run any beat under any experiment [`GATE-2-review.md` D1b]. All three layers are present at
every going-big moment; the table only names which leads.

| D1a context | Tier | Lead | Support | Composition | Text/voice at the beat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge-key payoff** — player connects a fragment across years (Teledahn cascade [`pnc-grammar` §2.2]) | 1 | **A** (the preload *is* the moment) | C quiet delivery; B palette lift | Large *or* intimate | Silent or one flat word [`voice-style-guide` §4] |
| **Retrospective-significance reveal** — an ordinary detail detonates [`voice-style-guide` §4] | 1 | **A + C** | B held-frame color | Usually intimate (the specific object) | No line; the player's pause is the *ma* beat |
| **Reincarnation echo lands** — deepest-bond motif, weight from another's attachment [`voice-style-guide` §4] | 1 | **A** | C stillness; B tight composition | Intimate zoom | Silent; the echo is the delivery |
| **Year-cycle / meta-layer reward** | 1 | **B** (the scheduled tableau swell [`myst-techniques` 2.9]) | A gates it (preload); C keeps text flat | Large-scale establishing, held | At most one plain NPC line |
| **New-room / reward-space reveal at a gate** [`myst-techniques` 3.10] | 1 | **B + C** (composed swell, held) | A authorizes via the gate's knowledge-key | Large-scale (the space is the payoff) | No UI, no narration |
| **Sprinkled wonder — mid-scene beauty** | 2 | **B** (ambient awe) *or* **C** (object specificity) | A reframes it as a wonder-seed | Both — intimate rewards *looking*, wide rewards *arriving* | Nothing; rewards the eye |
| **Year-to-year environmental shift** (delta) [`myst-techniques` 2.1] | 2 | **C** (delta as time-carrier) | B palette-state continuity across years | Background state change, no new room | No intertitle |
| **Scene-entry establishing beat** [`frieren-animation-breakdown`] | 2 | **C** (linger before action) | B composition amplitude | Wide "this is the world" shot | None; sets the register |
| **Visible-but-inaccessible mystery** — off-frame implication [`myst-techniques` 2.6] | 2 | **A** (produces the live question) | C ambient-life; B saturated lure-object | Either | None; the world is bigger than the frame |
| **Pack-triage / end-of-run** | 1 or 2 | **C** (quiet, ambient, no ceremony) | A if the run earned a revelation | Background state of the square carries it | The "Thanks." template [`voice-style-guide` §4] |

**Reading the distribution.** Experiment **A** leads wherever the moment is *earned by
accumulated knowledge*; **B** leads wherever the moment is a *scheduled, composed amplitude*
(the rare tableau); **C** leads wherever the moment is *ambient, entry-level, or a quiet
payoff* — which is most scenes. The default register across the whole game is C; A supplies
the mechanism that makes any payoff mean something; B is the rare, budgeted breath. That
distribution is the closest fit to Roc's stated lean — "an Outer-Wilds mechanism rendered in
a Ghibli register" [`GATE-2-review.md` D1] — but the brief does **not** lock it, because D1b
said experiment. Phase 3 can reassign any row.

## 5. How a going-big moment renders in a cozy static-scene P&C

A P&C game is a sequence of static or near-static backgrounds the player moves between. Every
experiment treats this as a *native fit*, not a constraint — the Ghibli/Frieren background
*is* a static composition, exactly what the game renders [`going-big-brief.lens-2-ghibli-awe.md`
§3; `going-big-brief.lens-3-frieren-restraint.md` §3]. A **Tier-1 payoff swell** has five
mechanical components, integrated from all three experiments:

1. **A silent before-beat.** No dialogue, no prompt. The scene's state shifts or a prior gate
   opens. The player understands what is happening before anything is said — because they
   carried the knowledge in (A). The game holds.

2. **A single legible state-change in the world.** One visually legible thing marks the
   convergence — a door now open, an object in a new position, a calendar date now annotated.
   *One*, not several — the single chokepoint unlocking the downstream chain
   [`pnc-grammar` §2.2].

3. **A new spatial reveal (where budgeted), composed as the swell.** The newly-accessible
   space is the reward, designed as a *destination*, not a corridor to the next puzzle
   [`myst-techniques` 3.10]. On the visual-amplitude reading (B) it gets a keystone-image
   composition, a saturation lift, and a held frame; on the restraint reading (C) it gets a
   wider, more-open composition held quietly with no saturation change. For an
   **intimate-zoom** version the "space" is a drawer, a journal, a face — the reveal is an
   object's depth, and the detail-gradient reverses locally so the object is fully rendered
   while the rest of the scene fades to atmospheric context [`frieren-backgrounds-analysis`].

4. **A minimal verbal acknowledgment (optional; flat register).** If a character speaks at
   all, the line is short, plain, and lands on the trailing beat — no announcement of
   importance. The emotion lives in the gap between the line and the new space, not in the
   line. A single word carries everything "if the setup trusted the player to remember"
   [`voice-style-guide` §4]. The two layers never amplify the same beat simultaneously (§2).

5. **Resumption at the quiet altitude.** The scene continues; the world does not dwell. The
   pacing-contrast rule means the decisive moment lands hard *because* the surrounding world
   is mundane and returns to mundane [`voice-style-guide` §6, `couple-seconds-to-end-a-fight`].
   The going-big moment is bounded; it does not become the game's new register.

**Tier-2 wonder seeds** are cheaper: a small object conspicuously out of place, a door newly
open, a distant structure whose function the player cannot yet read, a window with moving
curtains, a path leading off-screen. Each implies a world larger than the frame; the wonder
is *inference* — the player's mind fills the off-frame space with more world than the artist
rendered [`myst-techniques` 2.6, "cheap curiosity engine"]. The smallest animation additions
(a candle particle pass, a fountain ripple, cloth in a doorway) are the **quality floor**,
budgeted as baseline not stretch — the hardest thing to notice when absent, which is the point
[`frieren-true-beauty-retrospective`].

**Static-scene translation note.** "Held composition" is a film concept; the Frieren
reference is animated. For a truly static P&C the nearest equivalent is ambient animation
(particles, light flicker, environmental loop) + ambient audio — the player self-creates the
*ma* beat by pausing, and the design job is to make the background reward that pause
[`going-big-brief.lens-3-frieren-restraint.md` §3, §8].

## 6. Phase-3 dials that stay open (not resolved here)

These survive the brief as tunables, per the trade-offs the compare surfaced. None is a
number this synthesis should invent.

1. **Visual-amplitude ceiling at the payoff (B vs. C).** How much saturation lift and
   composition widening the cozy pillar can bear before the game stops being restrained. A
   per-scene dial, not a global value. Resolved by the §2 reading Roc picks, then tuned in
   scene drafts.

2. **Swell-frequency budget (untested).** L2 offers a starting hypothesis — "one payoff swell
   per major gate, two or three sprinkled wonders per festival-week run"
   [`going-big-brief.lens-2-ghibli-awe.md` §6] — carried here explicitly as *untested*. Too many
   swells erode the contrast that makes them work.

3. **The static-scene timing problem.** A visual swell needs the player in the scene long
   enough to register it; a fast player misses the held composition. The payoff-swell room
   must reward lingering *without* pushing the player — the guidance-vs-confusion dial
   [`voice-style-guide` §6]. Belongs in the pnc-grammar Phase-3 multi-goal-availability work.

4. **Preload-list fill order.** Place the Tier-1 revelation markers early (cheap); fill the
   preload lists *after* the calendar + scene maps exist, since a Year-4 detonation's preload
   depends on what the player saw in Years 1–3 [`going-big-brief.lens-1-ow-revelation.md` §6]. A
   sequencing instruction, not a blocker.

5. **Seed-to-detonation ratio.** The diegetic-noise ratio is a deliberate dial, not a maximum
   [`pnc-grammar` §3]. Phase 3 tracks which wonder seeds resolve into which payoffs so the
   sprinkled wonder doesn't degrade into sprinkled noise.

## 7. Thin spots / KB gaps (honest accounting for GATE 2)

- **The large-vs-intimate composition distinction rests on D1a directly, not on an
  independent KB technique.** All three experiments read it from Roc's decision
  [`GATE-2-review.md` D1a]; no KB note establishes it as a named craft move. Single-source by
  nature (it is a Roc-call). Phase 3 validates through scene drafts.
- **The layer-split reconciliation (§2) is the load-bearing editorial call.** It is
  well-supported — all three experiments gesture toward it — but it is an *interpretation* of
  `voice-style-guide` §4 as a *text* rule, not a quote from it. The §2 fallback (total-register
  reading → Experiment-C understated-but-vast) is fully specified so GATE 2 can choose.
- **No sourced audio grammar for any experiment.** What ambient sound does at a *ma* beat, how
  a festival mix carries wonder vs. announces it, is not in these notes; H15 is ○○○ in the KB
  [`coverage-map.md` §2 H15; `going-big-brief.lens-3-frieren-restraint.md` §8]. The sonic side of
  going-big is derived in Build §8/§7 from first principles or Roc's musical sense, not from
  the corpus. **Flagged thin — single-domain gap.**
- **The swell-frequency budget is untested** — L2's number is a hypothesis with no source
  [`going-big-brief.lens-2-ghibli-awe.md` §6]. Named as an open dial (§6.2), never fixed.
- **"Held composition" needs a static-frame translation.** The visual references are animated;
  the P&C equivalent is ambient animation + audio + player-paced *ma*. The method translates;
  the technique requires the translation step (§5).
- **Intimate zoom is sourced only at the principle level.** The KB establishes *why*
  small-object rendering works; *which* objects earn intimate zoom in this world are Roc's
  content calls (H1 NPC props, H3 seed items) and park in Phase 3
  [`going-big-brief.lens-3-frieren-restraint.md` §8].
- **This brief does not resolve D1b.** Per the locked inputs the pole stays **open to
  experiment** [`RESYNTHESIS-PLAN.md` §2; `GATE-2-review.md` D1b]. The routing table (§4) is a
  recommended distribution, not a lock. Roc chooses at GATE 2.
