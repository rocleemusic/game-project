# Going-Big Brief — Lens 1: Outer Wilds REVELATION

**What this lens argues.** In this game, "going big" is not a spectacle event. It is the
moment a season of small, ordinary observations detonates into one vast understanding —
the mechanism Outer Wilds calls its design spine and our KB grounds in two independent
streams: the knowledge-key metroidbrainia [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`] and
the retrospective-significance engine [`voice-style-guide §4`]. The bigness is cognitive
and emotional, not visual. A single word, a returned object, a calendar date that now
means something — these can be the detonation. What makes the moment "big" is not what
the game adds to it at the moment of payoff; it is what the player already carried into it.

**What this lens does NOT argue.** It does not say every moment of going big must be
quiet. Roc's D1a decision explicitly permits a swell at narrative and reward payoffs, and
sprinkled small wonder moments [`GATE-2-review.md D1a`]. This lens says the OW mechanism
is what *earns* that swell — you can amplify at the payoff only after the weight has been
preloaded through the accumulation of small, ordinary, seemingly-unconnected observations.
Amplification that is not earned by prior preloading destroys the effect it is trying to
celebrate [`voice-style-guide §4`].

---

## 1. What "big" means under this pole

### 1.1 The OW mechanism — accumulated small observations detonate into one understanding

Outer Wilds' progression is defined by a single axiom: "knowledge is how you progress in
the game" [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`]. The player can go anywhere from
the start. What changes is understanding. The detonation moment is not a new location or
a new item — it is the instant the player realizes that a dozen separate small things they
noticed were all telling the same story, and they can now *read* it.

Our game already has this engine installed. The Teledahn pattern in `pnc-grammar` §2.2
names it mechanically: "one elegant observation gates the whole downstream chain — a
single memory learned in Year 2 turns a dozen dead interactions across the week into live
ones." The Laki pattern (§2.5) names the travelling cipher: "knowledge learned anywhere
is usable everywhere." The revelation moment is when these click. It is, in Roc's
framing, the non-violent equivalent of the mage tournament — it gives contrast (quiet
default → rare spectacle) and an outlet to go big, while the tool of going big is
understanding rather than power [`GATE-2-review.md D1`].

### 1.2 Retrospective significance as the structural preload

The voice guide's engine is: "an ordinary detail becomes meaningful in hindsight" — and
the reason the voice stays quiet is that "a quiet surface is what lets a small thing
detonate later" [`voice-style-guide §4`]. This is not a coincidence with the OW
mechanism. It is the same mechanism, expressed as a prose register rather than a
navigation model. Small observations are seeded low, beside plot-inert noise
[`voice-style-guide §4`]; knowledge fragments are placed in the world to be discovered
by the player pursuing their own curiosity [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`].
The detonation is retrospective: it lands *because the player carried it*.

The metroidbrainia tableau in `myst-techniques` item 2.9 names the spatial form of this
payoff: "accumulate expectation across the whole Age, then state the theme as a physical
tableau in the final room." The reveal-space in `myst-techniques` item 3.10 names its
scope discipline: "budget at least one new spatial reveal per major gate — the new space
itself is the payoff, not a collectible or score." Under this lens, those two techniques
are already OW-revelation techniques. The tableau is the detonation point. The new space
is its embodiment in the world.

### 1.3 What the "big" moment consists of, under this lens

A revelation-mode big moment has three parts — all of which must be authored, none of
which happen at the moment of payoff:

1. **The preload phase** — small, ordinary, seeming-noise observations distributed
   across scenes and years. None of them signal their future importance. The player
   notices them or does not; the world keeps moving either way
   [`voice-style-guide §4: "plant low, beside plot-inert material"`].

2. **The convergence phase** — the player acquires one more piece (or revisits an old
   one in a new context), and the prior observations snap into alignment. This is the
   "aha" instant. It is not authored as a scene event; it happens inside the player
   [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds: "pull, never push"`]. Our calendar model
   makes this native: the same festival week replayed across years means the convergence
   can use time itself as the final axis of alignment.

3. **The acknowledgment phase** — what the *game* does at the moment of convergence.
   This is where the tension lives. Under this lens, the acknowledgment must be minimal:
   a plain word, a brief new view, an object now in a different state. The player has
   already felt the weight; the game only needs to confirm it.

## 2. WHERE it applies — payoff swell vs. sprinkled wonder

Roc's D1a decision names two application contexts for going big: **a permitted swell at
narrative and reward payoffs**, and **sprinkled small wonder / beauty moments**. Under this
lens, both exist, but they operate differently.

### 2.1 Narrative and reward payoffs — the sanctioned swell

A swell at a payoff is permitted exactly because the weight was preloaded. The swell
does not create the weight; it acknowledges it. Under this lens, the rule is:

> **A swell is earned only after accumulation. The test is whether the preload exists —
> not whether the moment deserves it in isolation.**

What the swell looks like in a cozy P&C scene is not an orchestral crescendo or a
full-screen effect. It is a beat of stillness after the reveal — the world holds. Then
the scene opens. New space revealed [`myst-techniques 3.10`]; the physical tableau
completes [`myst-techniques 2.9`]. This is what space can do that dialogue cannot: it
lets the player stand in the answer and look around. That standing-in is the swell.

The sanctioned swell moments in this lens are:
- The first time the player's accumulated knowledge allows them to pass a gate they have
  encountered multiple times across years (Teledahn cascade light-up [`pnc-grammar §2.2`]).
- The first time a seeded detail from Year 1 pays off in Year 3 or Year 4 via a different
  character's attachment to it [`voice-style-guide §4: "weight from someone else's attachment"`].
- The spatial reveal of a new room or view that could only exist *because* of what the
  player now understands [`myst-techniques 3.10`].

At all three moments, a beat of restraint (the player stands in the new space; the
game does not speak first) followed by a minimal acknowledgment is the correct register.
Then the scene continues at the usual quiet altitude.

### 2.2 Sprinkled small wonder / beauty moments — the low-cost revelation

The second application is scattered throughout the experience — small moments of
curiosity-ignition that promise a larger understanding the player does not yet have. Under
this lens, these are not decorative. They are the *preload engine in action*.

Outer Wilds calls these "observable, physical, show-not-tell events that immediately
produce a question" [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`]. The physical event pulls
the player into curiosity; the text rewards the player who follows. Translated to a cozy
P&C scene: a small object conspicuously out of place. A door that is newly open. A
calendar date circled in a note with no explanation. A character who refers to something
the player has not encountered yet with no elaboration. Each of these is a small wonder
moment — it costs almost nothing and seeds the curiosity that makes the later detonation
land.

These moments should *not* pay off immediately. Their function is to produce a live
question in the player's mental ledger. The Outer Wilds ship log discipline applies here:
"every text entry is distilled to only what the designer is 100% certain the player knows
from that one piece of text" [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds`]. Each small
wonder moment gives the player exactly the question, not the answer. The answer is
elsewhere, discoverable later.

### 2.3 Large-scale vs. intimate composition — both are valid under this lens

Roc's D1a explicitly says the swell can be "large scale OR intimate zoom (scene
composition either way)" [`GATE-2-review.md D1a`]. Under this lens, the composition scale
does not determine whether the moment is "big." The revelation is what makes it big. The
composition is the *frame* the revelation detonates inside.

**Large-scale composition:** a wide establishing view reveals that a space has changed
since the player last saw it — the Ahnonay object-state-as-time corollary
[`pnc-grammar §2.4: "place the same landmark in degraded states across years to imply
elapsed history"`]. The bigness lives in the realization that time has passed and the
player now understands why. The wide frame makes the *scope* of that elapsed time legible.

**Intimate composition (zoom):** a close detail on a single object — a mark, a date, a
name on something the player has passed many times — now means something. The frame is
tight because the significance lives in the particular, not the panorama. This is the
ring, the horn, the open shutter [`voice-style-guide §4 exemplars`]. Zooming in is not
diminishing the moment; it is making the specific object *the world* for that beat.

The choice between large and intimate is a scene-composition decision, not a hierarchy of
importance. Some revelations call for the wide frame (the scope of what the player now
understands is spatial); some call for the close frame (the revelation lives in one
specific, ordinary thing that was always there). Both can carry equal weight under this
lens. Phase 3 chooses per scene.

## 3. How it renders in a cozy point-and-click scene

Under the OW-revelation lens, the going-big moment in a cozy P&C scene has five
mechanical components:

1. **A silent before-beat.** No dialogue, no prompt. The scene's state shifts or a
   prior gate opens. The player understands what is happening before anything is said —
   because they carried the knowledge in. The game holds.

2. **A single legible state-change in the world.** A door that was closed is open. An
   object that was in one position is in another. A calendar date that was opaque is now
   annotated. This is the game's "confirmation" at the acknowledgment phase: the world
   changes one visually legible thing to mark the convergence. Not multiple things. One
   [`pnc-grammar §2.2: "the single chokepoint unlocks the downstream chain — one
   elegant knowledge-gate opens all downstream systems"`].

3. **A new spatial reveal (where budgeted).** After the state-change, the newly-accessible
   space is the reward. The player walks (or pans) into a view they could not have seen
   before. This space is designed as a destination, not as a corridor to the next puzzle
   [`myst-techniques 3.10: "reward-space as destination — the new space itself is the
   payoff"`]. For the intimate-zoom version, the "space" can be a drawer, a journal, a
   face — the reveal is an object's depth, not a room's expanse.

4. **A minimal verbal acknowledgment (optional; flat register).** If a character speaks
   here at all, the line is short, plain, and lands on the trailing beat
   [`voice-style-guide §2.1`]. No announcement of importance. The emotion lives in the
   gap between the line and the new space — not in the line itself. A single word can
   carry everything "if the setup trusted the player to remember"
   [`voice-style-guide §4: "weight is preloaded, not performed"`].

5. **Resumption at the quiet altitude.** The scene continues. The world does not
   dwell. Pacing contrast (`couple-seconds-to-end-a-fight` model in `voice-style-guide
   §6`) means the decisive moment lands hard *because* the surrounding world is mundane
   and returns to mundane. The going-big moment is bounded; it does not become the game's
   new register.

## 4. The tension with "weight is preloaded, not performed"

The voice guide's sharpest rule for payoff moments: "amplification at the payoff destroys
it" [`voice-style-guide §4`]. This lens must live with that rule. Here is how they coexist.

### 4.1 The rule is about announced importance, not about acknowledged weight

"Amplification at the payoff destroys it" targets the failure mode where the game
*announces* that a moment is important — swells the music, lingers on the camera, has a
character say "this changes everything." That is performance in place of preload. It
destroys the effect because performed significance signals that the player *should* feel
something, which is different from the player actually feeling it because the setup earned it.

The sanctioned swell under Roc's D1a is not that announcement. It is an **acknowledgment
that the preload is complete.** The difference is timing and authorship: the swell happens
*because the player's own understanding just changed*, not because the game decided this
beat should feel big. The player's felt convergence is the event; the game's swell (beat
of stillness, new space, one plain word) acknowledges the player's experience. It does not
perform it.

### 4.2 Where this lens can fail — the genuine risk

This lens can fail in one direction: over-amplifying a moment that was not sufficiently
preloaded. If the "big" beat arrives before the accumulation phase has done its work,
any acknowledgment reads as manufactured significance — exactly what the voice guide
warns against. The test is always: "has the player been carrying this? or is this the
first time they are encountering this weight?"

A secondary failure: amplifying too many moments, which erodes the contrast that makes
the technique work. Outer Wilds' design kept the major revelation moments rare and
separated by long stretches of ordinary exploration. Our cozy game has less playing time
but the same discipline applies. The sprinkled small wonder moments should be frequent;
the full detonation moments should be rare and feel earned.

### 4.3 The practical guard

> **Before any going-big marker is placed in a scene spec: name the preload.** Which
> observations, across which years and scenes, have the player been accumulating? If there
> is no preload list, there is no revelation — there is only performance. The preload list
> is the going-big authorization.

This guard converts the voice guide's rule from a prohibition into a design gate. It is
not "don't go big"; it is "show your preload before you go big." The preload is the
ticket.

## 5. What this lens contributes to Build GDD §7

The going-big trigger model for Build GDD §7 under this lens produces three things:

**A trigger taxonomy (two tiers):**
- **Revelation trigger** — a payoff swell, earned by a named preload (the accumulated
  observation list). Rare. Allowed to hold the scene momentarily before resuming.
  Composition choice (large or intimate) is per-scene. Flat verbal register if any
  dialogue at all.
- **Wonder seed** — a sprinkled small-wonder moment. Frequent. No payoff at the moment
  of encounter. Its job is to produce a live question, not an answer. Cost is near-zero.
  Should not feel like a reward — it should feel like a world that is bigger than the
  current frame.

**A preload discipline:** every scene spec that contains a revelation trigger must include
a "preload list" field — named prior observations (which scenes, which years) that earned
this detonation. If the field is empty, the trigger is not yet authorized.

**A composition flag:** each revelation trigger is tagged `LARGE-SCALE` (wide view,
changed landscape, spatial scope) or `INTIMATE-ZOOM` (close object, specific particular
thing). This is a scene-composition instruction to the art direction, not a hierarchy. The
narrative weight is the same either way.

## 6. Thin spots and flags

- **The preload phase is distributed design.** Seeding the observations that will
  detonate later requires the full scene-map to be spec'd before the preload can be
  verified. In Phase 3 the preload list for each revelation trigger depends on knowing
  what observations the player will have had access to — which requires the calendar and
  scene maps to exist first. **Flag:** place the revelation trigger markers early; fill the
  preload list after the scene map is in place, not before.

- **"Wonder seeds" must not over-promise.** A small wonder moment that promises a payoff
  the game never delivers reads as confusion, not cozy curiosity
  [`curiosity-driven-exploration-outer-wilds Watch-outs: "do not let pull/not-push
  collapse into sparse and confusing"`]. Phase 3 should track which wonder seeds
  correspond to which eventual revelation. The ratio of seeds to detonations sets the
  player's expectation; too many unresolved seeds reads as noise.

- **This lens is grounded in two KB streams (OW note + voice-style-guide §4) and the
  Teledahn/2.9/3.10 patterns.** No claim here rests on a single thin source. The
  weakest grounding is the composition distinction (large vs. intimate) — this is read
  from Roc's D1a decision directly [`GATE-2-review.md D1a`] and has no independent KB
  source confirming it as a technique. Phase 3 validates through scene drafts.

- **This lens does not resolve D1b (which pole leads).** Per the locked inputs, poles
  are left open to experiment [`RESYNTHESIS-PLAN.md §2; GATE-2-review.md D1b`]. This
  output presents the OW pole at its strongest; the compare pass (not this lens) weighs
  it against L2 and L3 and makes a blend recommendation. Roc chooses.
