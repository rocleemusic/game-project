### 8.5 The Narrative Process

The narrative is generated by a repeatable process, not written by one person holding the whole world in their head. The systematic steps come first, and the craft rules ride on top as checks on what those steps produce. The crew is the one in Section 11: one agent per job, each agent takes a prepared input and hands back a typed output, and Roc approves the result at the end of the chain.

#### 8.5.1 The generation procedure

**1. Intake.** Roc hands the crew three things: the soul roster, the reshuffle map (which fixed essence lands in which role this life), and the current world state. The Narrative Architect cannot invent a soul. The roster is a required input.

**2. Persona cards (Narrative Architect).** One card per soul. Every field on the card is tagged one of two ways, with nothing left ambiguous:

- Essence, which never changes across runs: essence_descriptor (one want, one behavior cluster, one way the soul contrasts the player's arc), trait_axes, suit_tag, one line the soul will not cross, one object they carry, voice_register.
- Role, rebuilt every run: role_tag and everything that comes with the job.

The trait_axes are independent. Knowing one soul's value on one axis (how they deflect, how precise they are, how they show warmth, how much they talk when something is heavy) tells you nothing about their value on another. Before locking a card, the Architect checks it against the rest of the roster so two souls do not drift into the same combination. Each card leads with the one signal a player can actually notice, and detail no player will ever perceive is cut. The suit_tag is how two souls get a deliberate connection instead of an accidental one.

The swap test keeps essence and role honest: reassign a soul's role, and everything essence-tagged stays exactly the same while everything role-tagged is rebuilt. A postman who used to be the blacksmith and still remembers the forge is a tagging mistake.

**3. Authored exceptions.** By hand, five to ten percent of the roster breaks the schema on purpose. Each break lives on the soul's persona_card as an authored_exception, with the in-character reason written into canon_flags. The recorded reason is what keeps the Verifier from flagging it, and it keeps the exception slot from sneaking a scripted emotional beat past the gate.

**4. Echo map (Narrative Architect).** An echo_template pairs a seed planted early with a payoff that lands later. Each one is built to one of three shapes:

- deferred-gap: a gesture cut off and waved away ("it's nothing"), paid off later.
- logistics-first: the mundane version stated first, the heavy truth revealed after.
- motif-rhyme: a phrase dropped in light, then reused almost word for word in a heavier moment.

Each template holds: seed_scene, seed_event (25 words or fewer), payoff_scene, payoff_condition, the soul who voices the payoff (a different soul from the one who planted the seed, where the shape allows it), and one plain line naming the point of it, so the reason survives to Roc's review. There is no emotion field on the template.

Placement rules:

- The seed sits next to plot-inert business (a chore, a price, a pest), and every seed ships surrounded by ordinary non-seed detail, so the player cannot tell the seed from the noise when it is planted. A seed dropped into a spotlit, single-purpose scene is rejected.
- The payoff_condition is a knowledge flag the player sets by proving something in the world, never a visit count. Firing a payoff just because the player was present performs a recognition the player never made.
- The prove act happens in the world: show the object, ask the pointed question, perform the rite. It is never a menu that announces a solved puzzle. The player reads the result from the soul's reaction, not from a score.
- A wrong guess draws an immediate, soul-specific correction (from the persona_card) that says what is off and stays open to another try.
- Every payoff passes a structural check at the automated gate: it is in character, and it never overturns a fact the player has already confirmed. Whether it reads as obvious in hindsight is a craft call, and that call is Roc's at the human gate, not an agent's.

**5. What ships with each batch.** The Architect sends three things with every batch:

- delta_rule: each scene adds exactly one new fact, and that fact rides in as the byproduct of an ordinary job (a favor, a chore, a disagreement), not a lecture.
- canon_flags: the locked facts and motif markers on a fact tier, personal opinions on a bias tier, and the exception list.
- a cut-priority tag on every echo and encounter (must-ship, losable, flexible), so the three-year slice still ships cleanly when the flexible tier gets cut for scope.

**6. Graph before prose.** Every scene gets a stable ID before a single line is written. Encounters are self-contained nodes, assembled by what is true (world state, what the player knows, bond tier), not strung on a quest tree. Each one is written to work from any starting point, including a player who walks in knowing nothing. Errands are built from the current world state using a small fixed verb set (collect, make, show or ask, use), and they appear as leads the player finds, never as a marker pointing the way. The festival and the year-end retrospective are single reusable structures, called once per festival-year. The orchestrator picks the next encounter by reading the state so far, and the only nudge it can make is to replan: surface a clue, move a soul. It never fails a player, blocks an action, or hides what the player did to force a payoff. The graph is greyboxed with placeholder lines first, and Roc reviews the structure before any voice goes in.

**7. Prose (Content Agent, one line-slot per call).** The Content Agent gets one persona_card, one echo_template, the scene_context (the scene's ordinary job, plus at most one bond-want stated as something the soul does), a tone from the fixed list [quiet, wistful, matter_of_fact, warm, distant], the soul's voice_register, and a word cap. Tone is handed in; the agent never picks or invents one. The agent pulls a two-turn dialogue template from the tone lookup and bends it through the card's signature, so no two souls fill the same template the same way.

It returns content_lines carrying speaker_id, tone, text, scene_id, echo_flag, canon_flag, and speaker_intent, a private note on what the line means from the speaker's side ("grief spoken as accounting"). speaker_intent is about the speaker only. No field anywhere in the pipeline names a feeling the player is supposed to have. The agent never sees the bond count and makes no structural calls. On a payoff scene (echo_flag set), the tone handed in is quiet or matter_of_fact, the word cap tightens, and the line states its fact and stops, or gets cut.

**8. Cross-run state.** Continuity between lives lives in the engine save, not in the narrative layer. The save holds each soul's bond count (a tally of actions: time spent, help given, festival choices honored), the essence facts the player has confirmed, and the list of seeds already fired. That state is summarized on a set schedule: squeezed into a small per-soul summary at each year-skip, folded into a run summary at the retrospective, so it never grows without bound. Before writing a later-life line, the Content Agent reads that summary, so the line refers back to the earlier life instead of retelling it. A fired seed is never replanted, and a confirmed fact is never contradicted. Per-run state (which scenes were visited, this life's role map) resets at the year-skip, and that reset is what drives the reshuffle.

**9. Verification (Consistency Verifier).** Reads each batch and flags it against the fixed checklist in 8.5.2. It only flags. It never rewrites, and it never judges whether a beat lands. No agent judges that.

**10. AI-tell purge.** The Content Agent's automated tell-and-drift pre-pass (Section 11, Agent 2) runs on every batch before the gate. It is a fixed-pattern tool pass, not a new agent. It scans for em-dashes, the banned words (leverage, utilize, seamlessly, effortlessly, innovative, transformative, robust), summary openers, any line that names a feeling, and any payoff line that swells. A hit blocks the write until it is cleared.

**11. QA (Agent 5).** Walks the compiled scene graph: every scene reachable, no soft-locks, no dead-ends. Both directions of every echo hold: no seed without a reachable payoff, and no payoff that can fire before its condition is met. Every scene resolves from the knowing-nothing start, and every closed thread teaches its own reason. It only flags.

**12. The human gate.** At the output, never mid-chain. Roc approves every line before anything is written. A flag goes back to the agent that owns it, routed through the orchestrator. Workers never call each other.

#### 8.5.2 Cohesion guardrails

The checklist is short and locked. Every check tests a structural fact about the batch, never an opinion about quality.

- **Essence vs role.** Every trait and every line is tagged essence or role. Essence comes through the reshuffle untouched; role is rebuilt. Role memory never rides along with an essence.
- **Superposition.** The bond count is never shown: no line, no meter, no hearts, no number. No line states or counts how a soul feels about the player, and no line states what the player feels at all. The only confirmable facts are essence descriptors. Bond shows up only as behavior (how warm a greeting is, where a soul chooses to stand), and stays too soft to read as a meter. No step in the process tilts content toward one ending. Community, diffuse, and solitary-or-release are all possible; none is the target and none is a failure.
- **Delta.** Every scene carries a one-sentence delta naming its single new fact. An empty delta, a repeat of an earlier one, or a reshuffle scene that re-teaches an essence the player already confirmed is rejected before it is written.
- **Knowledge travels.** Every payoff is gated on a fact the player deduced. A payoff gated on mere exposure is flagged.
- **Feedback vs motive.** Two rules, kept separate. Feedback is specific: every wrong action gets a local, in-voice response that says what is off and teaches, wrong essence guesses stay open to retry, and silence or a generic refusal is a failure. Motive stays open: no line fully explains why a soul is the way they are. Teach the interaction; leave the person open to interpretation.
- **Voice register.** Each soul's register is fixed on their card and rides into every call. Breaking it is a violation unless the soul is on the exception list with a recorded reason. A tone outside the fixed list is a violation.
- **Fact tier vs bias tier.** Two souls disagreeing about the same event is texture, not a bug. A line that contradicts a locked fact (an essence descriptor, a hard canon flag) is a violation. Motifs are checked only for their marker drifting, never for whether they resolved. An unresolved motif is fine. Forcing a motif closed is the failure, not leaving it open.
- **speaker_intent stays about the speaker.** The speaker_intent note describes what the speaker is doing under the words, never what the player is meant to feel, and no agent treats it as a target to hit. The Verifier flags any speaker_intent that names a player feeling or reads as a score.

#### 8.5.3 The register

All player-facing text is Frieren-flat.

- One clause a line, and let the follow-through fall flat. The payoff of a well-planted seed is often a single word.
- Show the behavior first, name it second, if ever. A trait is something a soul does before it is labeled.
- Feeling comes out in the wrong register: grief as bookkeeping, care as irritation. speaker_intent is how a wrong-register line gets checked for fidelity.
- A motif rhymes by reusing the seed's own words, not a paraphrase.
- Silence is a valid ending for a beat: a cut sentence, a non-answer, the next scene simply starting.
- No line names the feeling it is withholding. No payoff recaps its seed. The weight is loaded in the setup, and the payoff never turns it up.
- Register is locked per scene, not per line. A seed scene and its payoff scene are separate calls with separate tones.
- When a payoff has earned a swell, the swell goes in the picture or the sound: a wider frame, a moved object, a held note. The words at that beat stay flat, or go silent. The ink-and-html proof has no visual layer yet, so the words hold the line anyway and never grow to make up for it.
- A character's inner life shows up in another soul's mouth, in the world, or nowhere. Never in a narrator line, never in a system line.

#### 8.5.4 The constraint, stated

The pipeline scripts two things. The mechanical recognition: the essence-side deduction, where the player makes a claim, the world confirms it at a threshold the player can read through an in-world act, and wrong guesses teach. And the narrative text: every line is authored, checked, and reviewed.

It invites one thing: resonance. No agent, field, check, or number in this process claims to produce, predict, or measure what the player feels. The bond count tallies actions, never feeling, and is never shown. Only the essence deduction can be won; the bond is built, not found. The Verifier checks structure, QA checks reachability, and whether a moment lands is nobody's job but the player's.

We write stories. We do not script the payoff.
