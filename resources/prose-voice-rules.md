# Game-Project Prose Voice Rules

*Governs all human-readable document prose in this project: the gdd/ domain files, the pitch, notes, and dev-crew notes. This is separate from the in-fiction voice (`knowledge-base/synthesis/voice-style-guide.md`), which governs NPC and narrative content inside the game.*

*Adapted from Roc's writing guides (`BizDev/archive/roc-voice-general.md`, `BizDev/skills/brand-voice/roc/voice-rules.md`). The exemplar is Roc's own §1 elevator pitch, quoted at the bottom.*

## Core feeling
A thoughtful colleague showing you something concrete. Not presenting, not teaching, not selling. Specific enough that the reader pictures it and recognizes themselves in it without being told to.

## Register guards
- **Show, do not tell.** Walk the reader through what actually happens and let them draw the conclusion. Roc's pitch does this: "the npc who was the blacksmith may now be the postman, a friend may now be a brother." It never says "this rewards players who like surprises." It shows the surprise.
- **Specificity is the credibility.** Name the actual thing. Never write a phrase that sounds specific but names nothing, like "the cozy-emotional player who plays for the world and the people in it." If a clause has no picture in it, cut it or replace it with the concrete thing.
- **Emotional directness over clever phrasing.** "And honestly, it was a lot of fun" beats "the results exceeded expectations."
- **Plain prose.** No formatting theatrics. Bold and headers only where the format needs them, not for emphasis mid-sentence.
- **Short paragraphs, plain transitions.** "Then," "After that," "On a return."
- **State the fact, not the reasoning.** Write what is true, not why you wrote it or how it will be used. Cut "for aiming X," "this drops from the extracts," "the takeaway is." The reader needs the content, not the scaffolding around it.

## Punctuation and formatting
- **No em-dashes.** Use a comma, a colon, or a period and a new sentence. This is the single most common AI tell in our drafts, so read one pass just for it.
- **No emojis.**
- **Parentheses are fine** for a short aside that adds personality.
- **Person and tense.** Second person for the pitch ("you are a mage, you spend your days"). Present tense and descriptive for the GDD body ("the game does," not "the game will").

## Never use (AI tells)
- "excited to share," "thrilled to," "I'm happy to announce," any summary opener.
- "leverage," "utilize," "seamlessly," "effortlessly," "innovative," "transformative," "robust," "unique [X] landscape."
- "The key takeaway is," "This shows that," "At the end of the day," any corporate summary move.
- Vague superlatives with nothing under them ("amazing," "cool," "powerful," "immersive") unless the sentence names why.
- Claiming specificity instead of giving it.

## Dev-crew notes
Same rules, tighter. A dev-crew note states one internal fact plainly and stops. The `Dev-crew note` label already marks it internal, so never restate "this is internal" or "it drops from the extracts." One note, one job. Plain over polished, and a concrete list beats a smoothed-over "someone."

## The exemplar
Roc's §1 elevator pitch is the target register:

> "You are a mage in a Ghibli-warm magical world who has just arrived in a new town. You spend your days foraging, crafting, learning folk magic, and getting to know your neighbors... On a return to the game, you arrive but find that all the npcs have shuffled, for example: the npc who was the blacksmith may now be the postman, a friend may now be a brother."

Why it works: every clause has a concrete picture, it is second person, it shows the loop and the wow without labeling either, and it uses zero em-dashes. When a passage drifts from this register, it is usually because it started labeling ("players who love X") instead of showing.
