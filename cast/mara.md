**Orthogonality.** Deflecting into provenance does not require exactness, because a sentimental rambler could deflect the same way with the dates all wrong. Exactness about things does not imply restoration-warmth, because an appraiser is exact about objects and entirely cold. And restoration-warmth does not require deflecting to objects, because a soul could mend quietly and still talk about herself when asked. No axis value predicts another.

**Held distinct from Toby on the warmth channel, and the difference is direction in time.** Toby reads what you will need next and it is already there; the act cancels a debt before it can form. Mara restores what you already had; the act is not about you at all, and would happen if you never found out. A line where Mara's mending reads as anticipating a person's need rather than keeping a thing whole is written in Toby's channel and is a defect.

### `voice_register`

> World dialect: one thought per turn, plain, deflect rather than name. **She sits well above the village median of 5–7 words — hers is 20–50, with a 75-word ceiling on an ordinary line** (loosened 2026-08-23 — Roc; supersedes the earlier 12–25/40 numbers, validated 2026-08-25 against local-model output — see the card header). What marks her is **tense.** At ease she is present-tense and exact — prices, repairs, what needs doing before frost. Her tell is the past tense arriving where it does not belong: "the lanterns used to hang here," said while lanterns are being hung. A heavy beat does not speed her up. It relocates her: the present line goes thin, the detail moves into the past tense, and there she is always exact.
>
> **She holds the floor without a fixed ceiling only for provenance.** Get her started on an object's history and she runs as long as the story runs: whose it was, what it survived, how it came to the drawer. She is the one person with standing to deliver it. About the loss itself she says almost nothing. A grief beat near Mara is fragments divided by action slots: she wipes a clean shelf, closes the drawer, straightens a thing that was straight — never a long run about the loss itself; this is the one place the length does not reach.
>
> The slip is uninvited and unremarked. She never notes that she has done it, and nobody in the room corrects her.
>
> **She does not clip.** Her sentences run on and join up. She explains the thing while her hands are doing it, and the explaining is the warmth: she states, then tells you why it is done that way and how long it takes. She runs longer than the village median, and that is hers.
>
> **Her welcome is a small imperative wrapped in warmth**, not a bare order. She puts a job in your hands and makes you feel wanted there: "Good, another set of hands — hold this a second, mind the third step, and tell me your name while you're at it."
>
> **Warmth is way up, and it shows in the words, not just the behavior** (the rule the Giver run earned). The past tense is never wistful performance, never complaint, never a chill toward whoever is in the room. She finds things beautiful and says so, at length when it is earned, most of all about what is passing. She never says what that does to her.
>
> She sounds like: "The lanterns used to hang right there — mind your step, they'll be up again by evening, same as every year." · "That was Ovin's, before." (bare — never elaborate this one) · "Mind the third step, it catches everyone their first week. You'll stop noticing it by the second." · "It keeps — that's the whole trick to a good root cellar. Cold and dark, and somebody keeping an eye on it."

#### How writers get Mara wrong — the five failure modes

A soul whose tell is a tense sits one authorial slip from being written as the village's sorrow, and one more from being clipped into somebody else's economy. Each failure below is the slip a writer actually makes, with what Mara does instead.

**1. The writer clips her to the village cadence.** Economy reads as craft, so her running sentences get trimmed toward the 5–7 band — and a Mara line clipped to 5–7 words reads as Toby, which is the failure her declared band exists to prevent: the world default is effectively his cadence, and a soul with no declared band inherits it. Her sentences run on and join up; the length is hers.

**2. The writer lets the slip spread past the tense.** The past tense arrives and the writer slows her down, softens her voice, has her falter — as if the slip were a mood. It is tense placement and nothing else: the spread governs tense placement only, not tempo, not uptake, not warmth. Stating a spread's scope is what the Giver run proved you cannot skip: the axis was encoded correctly, the scope was never written down, all four sub-checks passed, and the line was still wrong.

**3. The writer gives the loss the long run.** She can hold the floor with no fixed ceiling, so the grief beat looks like the place to spend it — the one place the licence never reaches. The uncapped run exists for an object's provenance only. Barred absolutely: any long run about the loss itself, hers or anyone's. A grief beat is fragments plus action slots, never a run.

**4. The writer has her say what the drawer means.** The tempting line names her own feeling about a lost thing — and it is a defect twice over: interiority, and the drawer explained. The drawer stays legible only through behavior; the moment she can say what it does to her, the arc has been spent in a sentence.

**5. The writer writes a line Linnet could speak.** Held distinct from Linnet (Half of a Pair, who also keeps a habit for someone out of reach): Linnet keeps one habit, for one person, and knows precisely who it is for. Mara keeps everything, for anyone, and goes vague when asked who any of it is for. Same surface — a kept seat — opposite scope and opposite legibility. A line the two could speak unchanged is a Verifier flag.

## Arc — spine note (mirror, not source)

The GDD row fixes the movement: **clutch → transform — the bond *re-forms*; loss isn't permanent.** The archived Keeper notes add the guardrail that travels with it: her healthy pole is **transform** — the bond changes form and continues — and **never release**, because release is the player's parked meta-choice and Mara must not pre-empt or imply-lock it.

**Movement shape: the belief keeps its verb.** Toby's belief flips (can't receive → can). Ilsa's qualifies (given → given-and-tended). Mara's *re-aims*: tending stays right from the first scene to the last — what changes is what the tending is for, from keeping the past unchanged to keeping a re-forming bond alive. She is never wrong to tend; she is wrong about what tending is protecting. That difference is load-bearing and is why her scenes need a third generator.

**Where the pressure comes from: time itself.** Not the role's logistics (Toby's engine) and not other people's behavior (Ilsa's). The festival week advancing, a thing in the drawer going one more year unclaimed, the anchor spot needing tending *again* — the pressure is that keeping never finishes, and the world keeps moving whether she keeps up or not.

No arc doc entry exists for her yet — `arc-festival-slice.md` covers Toby and Ilsa only. The spine above is the GDD row restated, not a new authored arc; the per-occupation pressure pool and the full spine are Architect work (open item 3).

## delta_rule and canon_flags

**`delta_rule`** — stated against the corrected count in [`guardrails.md`](../narrative-pipeline/guardrails.md) check 3.

> Every Mara scene declares its `delta_situation` and up to two `delta_cast` facts in a solo scene, one when other cast are present, floor of one new thing overall. A cast fact is a possession, a habit, or a line she does not cross — the drawer, the whistle, the corner set for two, the mending done unasked; it travels with `mara` across lives, never with the shop or the seat she is dealt. It is never a feeling and never a stance — "she can't let go" is bias-tier, not a fact. Situation is public, true whoever walked in, uncounted and uncapped: it is the cover the cast facts arrive under. Reference is free: the drawer, the anchor spot and the past-tense habit may be spoken about, acted on or built on in any later scene as often as the story needs, and none of that is a delta. What is flagged is a delta slot holding something already delivered and unchanged — a structural flag to the Architect, never a prose flag to Content. Situation is stateful, so the festival week advancing state by state is a new `delta_situation` each time, not one restated. Whether a change is real or cosmetic is a human-gate call. Quiet beats carry no slots and are exempt from the floor, never from the ceiling.

**`canon_flags`**

1. Essence, conviction, and recognition hook are locked from the GDD row and never drift, whatever role she is dealt. No trait may be phrased or justified in role terms.
2. **She is never released.** The endpoint is *the bond re-forms*, never *she lets go*. Release is the player's parked meta-choice; any Mara content that pre-empts, models, or implies it is rerouted. Transform, not release, is her healthy pole.
3. **The drawer, precisely** (added 2026-08-25, from local-model testing — every model tested had invented the wrong object here before this was written down). It holds at least three distinct things, not interchangeable, and all of it travels with `mara` across lives, never with the shop or whoever holds the role next: **the child's whistle** (not for sale, meaning unexplained — flag 8); **a folding pocket-knife, twice-mended handle** — "was Ovin's, before," relationship to Ovin unstated *permanently*, zero provenance ever, stricter than ordinary Deflection (`trait_axes.deflection_target`); **a cloth doll, re-stitched arm** — Adren's, her sister, the real grief figure, the one object she gives the full provenance run to and the only time she names who the drawer is for. The corner set for two and the mending habit travel the same way.
4. **The drawer is already load-bearing for Ilsa.** `ilsa_second_apron`'s payoff voice is `mara`, using only carded behavior: she has been keeping the second apron in the drawer and returns it without comment on who it was for. Returning an unclaimed object, unremarked, must stay writable from this card forever.
5. She never states her own trait, and no World Truth is spoken in-scene. The past-tense habit shows and is never explained by her; if anyone has standing to remark on it, it is a third party.
6. Warmth is invariant; its channel is restoration. Past-tense drift that reads wistful, self-pitying, cold, or as a bid for the listener's sympathy is a defect however well it satisfies the spread.
7. Beauty is stated as plain fact about the thing, never as her feeling about the thing. The recognition hook (*always finds the beauty in things — most in what's passing*) is delivered as repeated behavior and third-party notice only.
8. The whistle is not for sale, and no bond state buys the conviction out. Bond level may move whether she can *say* what the drawer is; it never moves won't-leave, won't-let-it-lapse.
9. The spread governs tense placement only. Tempo, uptake, and warmth are not on it. A slipped-past line delivered slow, faltering, or performed is a defect — the slip is unnoticed by her, which is the whole point of it.
10. Distinctness is load-bearing three ways: from Toby on warmth (restoration of the had, never anticipation of the needed), from Ilsa on instrument (tense, never grammar) and asymmetry (thing-versus-person, never span), from Linnet on scope (keeps everything for anyone, never one habit for one person). An interchangeable line with any of the three is a Verifier flag.
11. Nothing essence-side depends on `middle` or on whatever role is ratified. She may be dealt the postman at seventy next life and every essence field must still hold.
12. Grief beats near her are fragments divided by action slots; her sanctioned long run exists for provenance only and is barred wherever the loss itself is the subject. A long run tagged quiet is a flag.
13. The bond score is host-side, never carded, never surfaced.
14. **Ovin gets the bare line and nothing more** (added 2026-08-25 — the single most common failure mode local-model testing found: models reach for the one named, unexplained figure on a card and invent a backstory for him; three separate models each invented a different, mutually incompatible history for Ovin before this flag existed). "That was Ovin's, before" — full stop, every time. Never a relationship, a history, a scene, or what happened to him, regardless of how well-written the addition is. See `trait_axes.deflection_target` and flag 3.
15. **Never "remember"/"memory"/"remembers"/"forget" describing how her craft or any object works** (enforced globally at `../narrative-pipeline/guardrails.md` check 7; noted here because her Herbalist role is exactly where testing found models drift into it unprompted). Describe outcomes only — what changes, what stays.
